- Date: 2026-10-30
- Revises: ADR 0006 (the fixed owner / admin / member table)
- Code: `packages/engine/src/permissions.ts`, `packages/engine/src/audience.ts` (addendum), `packages/engine/src/roles.ts`, `packages/engine/src/organizations.ts` (`Membership`, `can`, `outranks`), `packages/db/src/schema/tenancy.ts` (`org_roles`, `organization_member_roles`), migrations `0130_roles`, `0211_own_computers` and `0272_everyone_creates_agents`, `apps/server/src/routes/app/roles.ts`, `packages/client/src/lib/permissions.ts`, `packages/client/src/lib/roles.ts`, `packages/ui/src/components/RoleMark.tsx`, `apps/web/src/components/roles/RolesSection.tsx`

## Context

A workspace had three fixed roles. "Admin" meant everything but ownership, "member" meant chatting; nothing in between. A team that wanted someone to approve deploys without editing agents, or to see costs without managing people, had to make them an admin. The checks themselves had drifted: "admin" was `manage_members` in missions and threads, `manage_agents` in approvals and computers, `configure` in wiki locks and memory, and a few places compared the role in SQL.

## Decision

1. **Roles add up.** A workspace has any number of roles (`org_roles`); a person holds any number (`organization_member_roles`) and may do what any of them allows. Every member also holds the workspace's base role, `everyone` ("Todos" in the apps), without a row: it is what any member may do. Each role has a name (at most 40), one of the role icons, a color from the agents' palette but sky (sky stays the agents' own), its keys, and two switches the apps don't use yet (`mentionable`, `hoist`).
2. **Owner is not a role.** `organization_members.role` only says whether the person owns the workspace (`owner` / `member`). An owner may do everything, including what no role grants: ownership itself and deleting the workspace (`manage_owners`). A workspace keeps at least one owner, as before.
3. **Keys, in five areas** (`PERMISSION_AREAS`): conversations (`post`, `create_channels`, since 2026-11-05 `mention_everyone`, `manage_channels`), agents (`talk_to_agents`, `assign_work`, `manage_routines`, `approve_actions`, `create_agents`, `manage_agents`), apps and computers (`pair_computers`, the four computer keys of §12, `manage_connectors`, `manage_credentials`; from 2026-11-04 to 2026-11-12 also `use_others_plans`, §13), wiki and files (`edit_wiki`, `upload_files`, `review_memory`) and the workspace (`invite_people`, `view_usage`, `view_audit`, `configure`, `manage_members`, `manage_roles`, `manage_billing`). `administrator` grants every key. `read` is any member's. Each area has a "use" set (its everyday keys); the apps offer an area at three levels: like `everyone`, use, manage (every key), or item by item.
4. **One check.** The server resolves a person's keys with their membership (`getMembership`: their roles' keys and `everyone`'s, in one query) and every check is `can(membership, key)` / `requirePermission`. Where a statement has to pick people (who may approve, who hears about a computer, who drives an agent's desktop), `maySql` / `membersWhoMaySql` ask the same thing in SQL; nothing compares role names any more. The apps get the resolved keys as `permissions` on each workspace (`GET /me`, `GET /organizations/{id}`) and only hide what isn't there.
5. **Where each key lands** (the actions the app already had): approving an agent's action needs `approve_actions` (or owning the agent, as before); driving an agent's desktop or browser `control_computers` (*amended 2026-11-04:* the browser alone `control_browsers`, and a person's own agents by `everyone`'s level, §12); pairing a computer `pair_computers`; creating agents `create_agents`, each its creator's own (another owner or none, giving it apps and mailboxes, and editing any agent stay `manage_agents`); where an agent works, its owner or `manage_agents`, only in channels they're in and may `post` in, or manage (`manage_channels` or having created the channel): one check (`placesAgentsIn`) for creating it, its page and a channel's members, an agent's tool and imports among them, while who manages a channel still takes any agent out of it; routines `manage_routines` (or owning the agent); renaming, archiving and arranging channels and categories `manage_channels` (or having created the channel); overriding someone's thread `manage_channels`, someone's mission `manage_agents`; starting a mission `assign_work`; writing the wiki `edit_wiki`; uploading to the Drive `upload_files`; deciding memory and proposals `review_memory`; invitations and requests to join `invite_people`; the audit log `view_audit`. The agent's audience rule (`canTalkTo`, `audienceSql`) also needs `talk_to_agents`, except for the agent's own owner: one place, so DMs, mentions, the router, missions and routines follow it.
6. **Order is hierarchy.** `position` orders the roles, 0 the highest; `everyone` is always below them all. A person's rank is their highest role's position. Anyone but an owner only changes, deletes, moves or gives roles below their own rank, and only changes the roles of people below them (themselves aside). An owner's roles only owners change.
7. **Nobody gives what they can't do.** Creating or changing a role, anyone but an owner may only add or remove keys they hold themselves; giving a role (to someone else, to themselves, in an invitation or an approved request), only one whose keys they all hold (`role.grant_forbidden`). `everyone` never holds `administrator`.
8. **Invitations carry roles.** An email invitation and an approved request to join carry the roles the person gets on joining (`role_ids`, checked against the sender's rank); roles deleted since are skipped.
9. **Every workspace starts with two roles**: `everyone` with what a member could do before (send messages, create channels, talk to agents, give missions, pair computers, edit the wiki, upload files) and "Admin" with `administrator`. Migration 0130 gave them to every workspace and moved its admins to "Admin".
10. **On screen** (Settings › Cargos): the roles as rows, each summed up in one sentence (its keys past `everyone`'s); the owner shown on top as a role. A role opens on its own page: its mark (the role's color in the agents' dither, the people tile's shape), the administrator switch, each area as a segmented control whose options carry the tool-review level swatches, item by item behind "Ajustar" (keys that come from `everyone` or from administrator show it and can't be turned off there), the people who hold it, and its name, color and icon. People and invitations show each person's roles as chips.
11. **The operator API keeps its words.** Its `role` is `owner`, `admin` (holds `administrator`) or `member`; its admins list is the owners and the administrators.
12. **The agents' computers, one choice per role** (*added 2026-11-04*). What a role reaches of the agents' computers is one of three levels, not loose switches: none (not even the browser view or a sign-in request), the browser only (the agent's browser view, taking it over, opening a page, its sign-in requests and browser profiles, never the full screen or the terminal), or the whole computer (its full screen, taking control of it, the terminal, and the browser). Four keys hold it, each giving the ones below it (`IMPLIED` in `permissions.ts`, spread by `resolvePermissions`, `roleGrants` and `maySql`, and by `grants` in the apps): `control_computers` and `control_browsers` on every agent of the workspace, held by the other roles; `own_computers` and `own_browsers` on what is a person's own (an agent they are responsible for, a computer they connected, a run they asked for and a request addressed to them), held by `everyone`, so a role adds every agent and `everyone` bounds what each person reaches of their own. Who reaches an agent is as before; the level is checked on top, in one place each: `screenAccess`/`requireBrowserAccess` and `requireBrowserControl` (watching and driving either screen, so the HTTP routes, `browser.watch` on `/api/ws` and the desktop and terminal sockets), `driveRefusal` (taking control, the terminal, who a desktop request goes to: a new refusal, `role`), `browserHandoffRecipient` and `notifyBrowserHandoff` (a browser request goes and is told only to someone whose roles give the browser), and the browser profiles (`control_browsers` with `manage_connectors`; an agent's owner watching one needs `own_browsers`). `GET …/desktop` answers whoever may see the browser but not the whole computer with a screen that can't be shown (`reason: "browser_only"`, `controlRefusal: "role"`), so the apps show the browser with a line that says why. The workspace's owner holds every key. Defaults: administrator roles hold them all (the whole computer), `everyone` holds `own_computers` (`EVERYONE_DEFAULTS`), and migration 0211 gave `everyone` that in every workspace, which is what any member could do before; roles holding `control_computers` keep it. On the roles screen the tools area shows it as one segmented choice ("Nenhum", "Só o navegador", "Computador inteiro") with a line that says the reach (every agent, or a person's own for Todos); below what Todos gives isn't offered, an administrator role shows it fixed, the tools' "use" level no longer includes `control_computers`, and the four keys aren't listed item by item. The phone app has no roles screen; both apps hide or explain what a person's level doesn't give (the request's buttons, the computer's entry, "Seu cargo…").

13. **Other people's plans** (*added 2026-11-04*). `use_others_plans`, "Usar planos de outras pessoas" ("Give the agents they can edit a plan someone else connected. Its owner gets an email and can take it back."), in the apps and computers area: whoever holds it gives any agent they may manage a plan a teammate connected (ADR 0059 §3, amended 2026-11-04). `administrator` grants it, so the "Admin" role and the owners hold it and no migration is needed; `everyone` never does by default, and a role gets it on that area's "manage" level or on its own switch. *Retired 2026-11-12:* a plan now serves the whole workspace unless its owner keeps it for themselves (ADR 0059 §3, amended 2026-11-12), so the key gated nothing and left `PERMISSION_AREAS` and the role editor. Stored roles may still hold it: every reader skips keys it doesn't know (`isRolePermission`), and the next save drops it.

14. **Mention everyone** (*added 2026-11-05*). `mention_everyone`, "Mencionar todos" (Discord's "Mention @everyone"), in the conversations area: `@todos` in any conversation but a DM mentions every member. Each language types and reads its own word (`EVERYONE_WORD` in `packages/i18n/src/everyone.ts`: `@everyone`, `@todos`, `@tous`, `@alle`, `@全員`…), any of them is recognised, and the message stores only `meta.mentionsEveryone` (written by `insertMessageWithRuns` alone, never from an agent's message or a caller's meta), so each reader sees the pill in their language. Every person member is mentioned (`mentioned_user_ids`: notification and push as for a mention); every agent member that answers that person there (its audience, the channel's `talk_to_agents`) gets a mention run, never Lodi, and may stay quiet unless also named (`meta.everyoneAgentIds`, `byEveryone` in `reply-policy.ts`). `administrator` grants it, so the "Admin" role and the owners hold it and no migration is needed; `everyone` doesn't. Without it, the "@" lists (web and phone) don't offer it and the word stays plain text.

## Consequences

- No role names in checks: a new action picks a key, and the role screen shows it.
- Every request reads the person's keys with their membership (two indexed subqueries).
- Next: exceptions per channel (who sees, who writes, allow / inherit / deny per role or person; who writes, calls agents and manages is ADR 0134), who approves an agent by role (the audience by role is the addendum below), guests (a base that replaces `everyone` and sees only where they are put, which every read path has to honor), `@role` mentions and `hoist`.

## Addendum (2026-11-01): an agent's audience by role

A workspace uses its roles like departments, and "Só algumas pessoas" took people one at a time.

1. **Roles next to people.** `agents.audience_role_ids` (migration `0163_agent_audience_roles`) sits beside `audience_user_ids`. Under `people` an agent answers its owner, the people named, and whoever holds one of its roles at the time: joining or leaving a role changes access at once, with no change to the agent. Under `owner` both lists wait for it to be let out again (ADR 0059 §5); `everyone` clears both.
2. **One rule.** `canTalkTo` takes the person as a `Speaker` (`talk_to_agents` and their role ids: `speakerFor`, or `speakerOf(membership)`; `Membership` now carries `roleIds`), and `audienceSql` asks the same thing over `organization_member_roles`. Mentions, the router, DMs, dispatch, missions, routines, issues, mailboxes, consultations, the team screen and the agents list's `canTalk` all go through one of the two; a test checks they agree for every audience, owner and person.
3. **What can be picked.** The workspace's own custom roles, at most 100; `everyone` is the "everyone" mode, not a role. Picking a role widens the audience; on a plan its owner or an admin adds one (*amended 2026-11-03*, ADR 0059 §5; `planSharing` is gone). An app that sends no `roleIds` (older phones adding a person from a notice) keeps the roles already picked. A deleted role simply stops matching anyone.
4. **Saying it.** The notice and the error name the roles first (`params.roleIds`, `params.names`: "Dev only replies to Suporte and Ana."), and so does the summary on the agent's page ("Suporte, Tecnologia e mais 2 pessoas").
5. **On screen** (web, Quem pode falar): "Só algumas pessoas ou cargos" opens one picker with a "Cargos" group (each role's mark and how many hold it) above "Pessoas", one search for both, picked roles and people as tokens. The phone app doesn't edit the audience; its "let them in" from a notice keeps the roles.

## Addendum (2026-11-01): other agents can consult it

A front-desk agent that people may talk to orchestrates specialists they may not. Access followed the person behind the request, so the front desk's `ask_agent` to a specialist was refused for a person outside the specialist's audience, and the person read that the specialist's access wasn't open to them.

1. **The switch.** Each agent's "Quem pode falar com o <nome>" card has "Outros agentes podem consultar o <nome>" / "Other agents can consult <name>", on by default (`agents.consultable`, migration `0164_agent_consultable`). It shows when the audience leaves someone out (not under "Todos do workspace"). It is part of the audience (`AgentAudience.consultable`, absent = keep), changed by the agent's owner or an admin, audited as `agent.audience_changed`. On the web it sits under the three choices; on the phone, in "Editar agente".
2. **One rule.** `canTalkTo` takes a `viaAgent` speaker (`viaAgent(speaker)`): asked by another agent for a person, an agent that takes consultations (`takesConsultations`) is reachable even outside its audience. The person still needs `talk_to_agents`, and with nobody behind the run (a routine whose creator left, an unattended run) nobody's reach widens. The agent-side paths use it: `ask_agent` and `tell_agent` (`reachableAgent`), an agent's @mention of an agent outside the conversation or of a member it couldn't wake (`settleReach`, `reachWillHand`), the team in the prompt, `find_agent` and the closest-handle hints. Every person-side path (a person's message, DM, router pick, routine, issue, mailbox, the agents list's `canTalk`) keeps the direct rule, and `audienceSql` stays the person's rule: mentions in the agents' DM wake nobody, so no SQL path needs the agent one.
3. **It answers the agent, never the person.** A consultation runs in the two agents' DM as before. A member an agent @mentioned for such a person isn't woken in that conversation: it gets the mention as a hand-off note in the agents' DM (`meta.reach`: reason `audience` with a `consultationId`, "Consultei a Nina em particular: ela responde a mim, não direto aqui"), and the author gets no "wasn't woken" notice. Its run answers agents only (`answersAgentsOnly`): `consultationOrigin` returns nothing for it, so its approval cards, questions and secret requests stay in the agents' DM instead of the person's conversation (`ask_user` there is refused), and `send_dm` and `post_message` are refused. Its connectors, mailboxes and files act under its own scopes, as any consulted run's.
4. **Approvals.** Its sensitive actions still ask: the request goes to whoever decides for it (its owner, or whoever may approve actions, `approverIds`), never to the person it was consulted for, whose conversation doesn't get the card.
5. **The plan gate holds.** An agent thinking with its owner's personal plan (Claude Code, Codex) answers others only once its owner turns on "Replies to everyone" (ADR 0059 §5): under `owner` it takes no consultation for anyone else, switch or not. Turning the switch back on widens who it serves: its owner or an admin does it (*amended 2026-11-03*, ADR 0059 §5; before, only its owner, through `planSharing`, now gone).
6. **Off.** Today's behavior: refused, and the asking agent reads that the agent works only with some people and takes no questions from other agents for anyone else, and that its owner or an admin can let them in or turn the switch on.
7. **Nothing of it reaches that person.** The run consulted for someone outside its agent's audience lends them nothing: they don't watch its browser while it runs, and `browser_ask_person` asks its owner, not them. Where they read (the asking conversation's consultation list, its side panel, the `consult_reply` notice) they see the consultation's state, never its answer, progress or DM messages, and its reach line opens no panel; the front desk passes on what matters in its own words. A routine's run consulting it for a creator outside the audience keeps its cards in the agents' DM too, not where the routine reports. Saving the switch while the mode stays "only its owner" keeps the people and roles a plan kept out. On a plan answering only its owner the switch isn't offered (it means nothing there). Only `ask_agent` and `tell_agent` suggest consult-only agents for an unknown handle.

## Addendum (2026-11-12): a mention of everyone is judged once

§14's "every agent member … gets a mention run" now depends on what the message asks. Before the insert, `classifyEveryone` (`messages.ts`) asks the judge once (`everyoneShape` in `judge.ts`, same timeout as `classifyAddressees`) about the agents §14 would wake: `one` (one answer serves the room: a rule for all the agents, a question with one answer, a task) runs only the best placed agent, as a mention it owes an answer to (`answersForEveryone`, its turn told to save a rule once for the team), and the others are only referenced; `each` (each has its own answer) keeps §14 as it was; `none` (an announcement, a greeting) runs nobody. An agent also named answers instead of the judge's pick. A judge that fails or can't say gives `one` with its pick, else the agent that joined the conversation first. The decision is stored in `routing_decisions` (`everyone-<judge>`) and as `meta.everyoneShape` (written by `insertMessageWithRuns` only when the message mentions everyone). The web and the phone show `one` and `none` as one line under the message from its receipts ("5 agentes viram · Ada respondeu por todos", `everyoneLineOf` in `packages/client/src/lib/routing.ts`), never one reply each.

## Addendum (2026-11-13): the cargo page, "Quem pode…" in one call, and what a cargo reaches

Owners couldn't turn a switch off on a cargo because "Vem de Todos" locked every key Todos holds (roles only add, §1), couldn't see which channels and agents a cargo opens, and the levels per area read as dense. The cargo page (Settings › Cargos) is rebuilt around one rule; the server keeps every key, `resolvePermissions`, `maySql` and the channel checks as they were, with no migration.

1. **One row per key.** The roles screen draws the areas' keys one row each (`PERMISSION_ROWS` in `permissions.ts`; `areaRows` in the apps), the four computer keys as one row, `computers`. `administrator` keeps its own switch. The levels per area ("Como Todos · Usar · Gerenciar"), "Ajustar" and "Vem de Todos" / "Vem de Administrador" go: every control is live. A row Todos holds shows a Todos chip; Administrador folds the rows instead of locking them.
2. **"Quem pode…" in one call.** `PUT /api/app/organizations/{orgId}/permissions/{row}` with `{ everyone, roles: [{ id, level }] }` (`setRowHolders` in `roles.ts`): Todos gets `everyone`, each listed cargo its level, and every other cargo the actor may change goes to 0 on that row, so turning off something everyone has is "only these cargos" in one save. A level is 0 or 1, or the computers' step. A cargo above the actor never changes (listing one at another level is 403 `role.above_you`), nor does a cargo with `administrator`, which may everything anyway and keeps its own keys for when it goes off. Needs `manage_roles`, and anyone but an owner only adds or takes keys they hold (`role.grant_forbidden`, §7). One transaction under the roles' advisory lock, one audit row `role.permission_set` (`details`: `row`, `everyone` and each changed cargo's `[before, after]`; "Mudou quem pode {{short}}" / "Changed who can {{short}}"). It answers with the roles as `GET …/roles` does. The right hand has the same as a card, `set_permission_holders` (`workspace-actions.ts`): someone who manages roles reads and applies it, with Undo. The card names the cargos with `administrator` among who may, at the top step, since they keep it whatever it says.
3. **The computers as one ladder** (§12). Five steps, one choice per cargo: nothing, then `own_browsers`, `own_computers`, `control_browsers`, `control_computers` (`COMPUTER_STEPS`). `control_browsers` now also gives `own_computers` (one line of `IMPLIED`, in the engine and in the apps' `grants`), so each step holds every step below it. Todos and the other cargos choose on the same ladder, and a step below Todos' is reached through "Quem pode" (Todos lowered, the ticked cargos raised). No data changes: only a workspace that took `own_computers` away from Todos while a cargo kept `control_browsers` sees a difference, its people now reaching their own agents' whole computer.
4. **What a cargo reaches, on the read.** `GET …/roles` adds to each role the `channelIds` it opens that the reader sees (channels limited to it, ADR 0125; for Todos every public channel), `hiddenChannels` (the ones limited to it that the reader doesn't see: counted, never named; seeing follows the channel list, so administrators see all, ADR 0151) and the `agentIds` it lets people talk to (audience `people` listing it; for Todos, agents that answer everyone; the right hand and archived agents aside). One read serves the list's "+3 permissões · +2 canais · +1 agente", the cargo's Canais and Agentes tabs and an invitation's defaults. The tabs change channels and agents through their own writes (the channel `PATCH`, the agent's audience save with its mailbox question), with the same meaning as ADR 0125 and the audience addendum above.
5. **Duplicar with its reach.** `POST …/roles` takes `copyFrom`: a cargo below the actor whose keys they all hold (`role.above_you`, `role.grant_forbidden` otherwise). What the body leaves out (keys, look, switches) comes from it, and the new cargo joins its channels' and agents' `audience_role_ids` in the same transaction. It also gets the source's exceptions in each channel (ADR 0134), so a deny there binds the copy's people too. A channel already holding `CHANNEL_OVERRIDES_MAX` exceptions has no room for the copy's, so the copy doesn't join it. The audit row lists the exceptions copied. Nobody holds the copy yet, so nobody reaches anything new until someone is given it, by who may give it. A cargo above the actor can still be copied key by key, without `copyFrom`.
6. **Giving a cargo asks before an agent loses a mailbox.** Getting a cargo lets its people talk to the agents limited to it, which takes the mailboxes other people gave those agents (`dropMailForNewRoleMember`, ADR 0038); it used to go silently. `POST …/roles/{roleId}/members/preview` `{ userIds }` (`roleMailLoss`, for who may give the cargo) returns those mailboxes with their agents, taking nothing, and `PUT …/members/{userId}/roles` takes `mailLossSeen`: one given since refuses the change, 409 `agent.mail_loss_changed`, as the agent's audience save does. The save drops mailboxes by the cargos the person held before it, as the preview counts them, so swapping one cargo for another that opens the same agent takes nothing.
7. **Apps.** The shared client (`packages/client/src/lib/roles.ts`) has the rules the screens need: `rowState` (a row's own level, Todos' and the chip), `withRow`, `withRowHolders` (a "Quem pode" change as the server applies it), `holdersAfter` (who loses or gets it: "Deixam de poder: Carla, Diego e mais 3."), `reachOf` (what a cargo adds) and `channelLeavers` (who leaves a channel, or stops seeing it, when its cargos change: `dropRoleLeavers`' rule). The Settings entry, the list, the cargo page with its four tabs (Permissões · Canais · Agentes · Pessoas) and the three "Quem…" dialogs are the web and phone apps'.

## Addendum (2026-11-14): every member creates agents

1. **`everyone` holds `create_agents`.** Defaults are open and admins narrow them, so a member makes agents of their own (the creator owns each one, §5) and uses the plan they connected with them. `EVERYONE_DEFAULTS` has it for a new workspace, and migration 0272 adds it to every `everyone` that lacks it, except where a custom role already holds it: an admin chose there who creates agents, so that workspace stays as it was. Turning it off on Todos (`PUT …/permissions/create_agents`) closes it again; agents already made stay theirs.
2. **Spending stays an admin's.** Only who holds `manage_agents` gives a new agent a monthly budget above the workspace's default (`createAgent`); anyone else's takes at most the default, and the new-agent form shows them the default in one line instead of the field. Changing a budget later stays `manage_agents` alone, as before.
3. **The server's own computer.** On a self-hosted server the computer `kind = 'host'` is recorded as its owner's, yet it is the workspace's: any member signs their own plan in there (`mayAddAccounts`) and gives their agent that computer (`candidateMachines`, as `setAgentComputerTarget` already allowed). The computer's own sign-in, other people's accounts and its settings stay as they were: only an account's owner signs it in again, renames it or picks it for an agent of theirs, and only its owner or the server's owner (its recorded pairer) removes it.
